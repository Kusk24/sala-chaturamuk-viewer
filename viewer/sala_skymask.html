The same photographs and the same optimiser as the run beside it, with one change: a segmentation network labels the sky in every frame and those pixels are excluded from the loss, so the optimiser is never rewarded for building geometry there. 597,067 Gaussians after 30,000 iterations. Both runs are kept, because the change is small on the building and large in the empty space around it, and only a pair shows that.

The headline number falls, and that is expected. Scored the standard way, over every pixel of 50 held-out photographs, this run reads 14.45 dB against 23.45 dB for the run beside it. The model was trained not to put anything in the sky, so it renders nothing there, while the photograph it is compared against is roughly 28% sky. The score is measuring an absence that was asked for.

Scored on the pavilion only — the same pixels, the same 50 photographs, both models — it is 22.61 dB against 22.34 dB, better in 42 of the 50 views. A real improvement, but a small one: masking the sky does not make the building sharper, it stops the optimiser spending Gaussians on something it can never get right.

Where it actually pays is the empty space. Projecting every Gaussian back into the 50 held-out cameras and asking how often it lands on sky rather than on the building: the number that sit in sky in at least half of those views drops from 632 to 34, and at a looser threshold from 2,285 to 501. Weighted by size and opacity — how visible a floater actually is — the share of the model occupying sky roughly halves, 0.95% to 0.49%. The size filter also had less to remove afterwards, 4,756 oversized Gaussians against 6,435.

How it is done matters more than that it is done. An earlier run painted the sky black, and lost 2.2–2.4 dB: a colour rule decided differently from frame to frame, so the model was told the same sky was black in one photograph and blue in the next. Here nothing is painted. The mask is carried as an alpha channel, the render is multiplied by it before the loss is taken, and the photograph itself is never altered — so a masked pixel contributes no gradient at all. A pixel masked in one view and not in another is one fewer observation rather than a contradiction, which is why an imperfect mask is survivable here and was not before. The sky boundary is eroded a few pixels into the sky, so a wobbly edge costs a thin rim of sky rather than the roofline. Sparse points seen in sky are dropped before training starts, 1,409 of 240,357.

What it does not fix. Some haze remains around the roof. Those are Gaussians attached to the building's own silhouette, seen against the building in most views and against sky in only a few, so the mask never suppresses them. Zero gradient in the sky also means zero penalty, so a Gaussian that drifts into open air is not pushed back out — the crop and the size filter still do that work afterwards. Two limits are untouched because they live in the photographs, not the loss: no camera looks down on the roof, so nothing can invent what was never photographed, and COLMAP still files the 51 Sala Inside frames as a separate sub-model that this run does not include.
